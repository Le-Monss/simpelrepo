Kevin Mitnick

a.k.a. "Condor"

1963 – 2023 · Los Angeles, California

For two and a half years the FBI chased a hacker who was, in his own words, playing a version of the video games he'd loved since childhood — except the board was the American phone network, and getting caught meant prison. This is the story of the games that were named after him, and the game he said he was playing all along.

// 01 — THE GAMES

Three things called "the Mitnick game"

His story didn't just get written down — it got named, replayed, and eventually turned into training material. Here are the three places the word "game" attaches to his name.

1983 · FILM

WarGames

The teenage-hacker thriller starring Matthew Broderick arrived while Mitnick was still a teenager himself, and rumors that he'd broken into North American Aerospace Defense Command systems followed him for years afterward. He always denied it, but the legend stuck hard enough that the film is still the first thing many people mention alongside his name.

1996 · BOOK

The Fugitive Game: Online with Kevin Mitnick

Journalist Jonathan Littman spent nine months on the phone with Mitnick while he was a federal fugitive, then turned those calls into a book about the manhunt. The title frames the chase itself as a game — Mitnick building new identities, the FBI and rival hackers closing in, both sides treating the pursuit as a contest with its own rules.

PRESENT DAY · TRAINING

Social-engineering "games" in security training

After his release, Mitnick rebuilt himself as a security consultant and speaker, demonstrating the same manipulation techniques he'd once used against companies. That work fed directly into the cat-and-mouse exercises — red-team drills, phishing simulations, capture-the-flag events — that security teams now run under names openly borrowed from his career.

// 02 — TIMELINE

How the chase unfolded

1988

First real conviction

Arrested for taking proprietary software from Digital Equipment Corporation. Sentenced to a year in prison plus three years of probation.

1992

Goes underground

A new warrant is issued for a probation violation. Rather than turn himself in, Mitnick disappears — the start of the run that "The Fugitive Game" documents.

1992 – 1995

Two and a half years on the run

Mitnick works ordinary jobs under invented identities — a law firm, a hospital — while continuing to access networks he shouldn't. He later described the whole period as feeling like a video game he couldn't stop playing.

Feb 1995

Capture

Tracked down with help from security researcher Tsutomu Shimomura, whose own book "Takedown" tells the pursuit from the other side. The arrest makes front-page news worldwide.

2000s – 2023

From fugitive to consultant

After his release, Mitnick founded a security firm, wrote several books, and spent two decades teaching the techniques he once used to break in. He died in July 2023 after a battle with pancreatic cancer.

// 03 — LEGACY

Why the "game" framing stuck

  • He said it himself. Mitnick repeatedly compared the manhunt to a video game — a dangerous situation he'd engineer, then try to escape.
  • His crime was rarely damage. By his own account he was after access and knowledge, not money — closer to besting an opponent at chess than to theft.
  • The chase had two authors. Both Littman's book and Shimomura's "Takedown" tell the same pursuit from opposite sides, reinforcing the idea of a contest with two competing players.
  • He later ran the game in reverse. As a consultant, Mitnick got paid to break into companies on purpose — turning the exact contest that once made him a fugitive into a teaching tool.
